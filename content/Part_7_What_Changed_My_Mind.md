# Part 7 — What Changed My Mind?

## A. What I Believed Before

Before starting this investigation, I believed that imperial China's examination system was relatively fair because candidates were expected to follow the same rules. I thought that if someone studied hard and performed well, they could improve their social position. This seemed reasonable to me because the examinations appeared to reward knowledge and effort rather than simply allowing people to inherit government positions.

However, I had not considered whether everyone had the same opportunities to receive an education and prepare for these examinations.

## B. What Challenged My Thinking

When I first asked ChatGPT, it explained that examinations created opportunities but that family resources and personal connections could also affect success. This made me question my original assumption, but I wanted to check whether these claims were supported by historical evidence.

The research by Jiang and Kung (2021, Evidence 02) was especially important because it found that fathers' education predicted metropolitan examination success, while fathers' and ancestors' official status helped predict final palace examination rankings. Their direct wealth measures were not similarly predictive. This helped me understand that family advantages were not only about money. Educational knowledge and family background could also influence a candidate's opportunities.

Bai and Jia (2025, Evidence 03) further challenged my thinking by showing that both examination performance and connections to influential examiners mattered for reaching higher official positions. The journal issue is dated 2025, while the publisher records its first online publication on September 15, 2026. I realized that passing an examination and achieving a successful official career were two different things. However, these connections were not necessarily evidence of corruption.

The 1711 Kangxi edict (Evidence 05) also changed my perspective. It showed that the emperor was concerned about examination procedures in which some papers were not carefully reviewed and favored candidates could receive preferential attention. This demonstrated that having formal examination rules did not always guarantee fair assessment. However, one edict cannot establish how widespread these problems were.

## C. How My Understanding Changed

My opinion did not completely reverse. I still recognize that examinations created genuine opportunities for some educated men to advance. However, I no longer think that following the same rules automatically meant having equal opportunities.

I now understand that candidates could begin with very different educational backgrounds and levels of preparation. Passing an examination was also different from obtaining an official position or advancing to a higher government position. Merit and family advantages could operate together within the same system.

I also learned to be more careful about historical scope. Some of the research I examined focuses specifically on Qing candidates, including detailed evidence from the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Therefore, I cannot automatically assume that all these findings applied in exactly the same way throughout the Ming and Qing dynasties.

## D. What I Learned From This Investigation

This project taught me to question historical explanations rather than accept them simply because they sound reasonable. ChatGPT helped me identify important issues and organize my initial ideas, but its claims needed to be checked against academic research and a primary historical document.

I learned to distinguish evidence from interpretation, recognize the limitations of historical sources, and avoid making conclusions that go beyond what the evidence can support. I also realized that one source cannot explain an entire historical system.

Most importantly, I learned that understanding history requires asking not only what rules existed, but also how those rules affected people with different opportunities. I now understand that AI can be a useful starting point for historical research, but developing my own judgment and checking historical evidence are essential to reaching a reliable conclusion.
