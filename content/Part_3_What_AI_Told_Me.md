# Part 3 — What AI Told Me

**AI Tool:** ChatGPT (GPT-6)

**Prompt:** Did the imperial examination system in China provide equal opportunities for people from different social classes, or did it mainly benefit wealthy and educated families? Explain how the system affected social mobility and inequality, particularly during the Ming and Qing dynasties.

**Summary of AI's Response:**

According to ChatGPT, the imperial examination system in China had both positive and negative effects on social mobility.

The system allowed some men from families without a tradition of holding government office to gain official positions through their examination performance. This created opportunities for social advancement that were not based entirely on inherited status.

However, the examination system did not provide equal opportunities for everyone. Wealthier families generally had greater access to education, books, teachers, and time for examination preparation. This gave them advantages over people from poorer backgrounds.

Furthermore, success in the examinations did not always guarantee an official position. Family background, personal connections, and the limited number of available government positions could influence career opportunities.

**Three Main AI Claims to Investigate:**

1. The imperial examination system created opportunities for social mobility by allowing some people from non-official families to enter government service.

2. Wealthier families had greater advantages in examination preparation, making success more difficult for people from poorer backgrounds.

3. Passing the imperial examinations did not guarantee equal career opportunities because family connections and access to official positions also mattered.

## From AI Response to Historical Investigation

After receiving ChatGPT's response, I realized that its explanations needed further examination. Although the claims sounded reasonable, I wanted to determine whether historical evidence actually supported them.

In Part 4, I examine academic research and a Qing primary historical document to investigate these claims. In Part 5, I evaluate what ChatGPT explained correctly, what it oversimplified, and what required further evidence.
